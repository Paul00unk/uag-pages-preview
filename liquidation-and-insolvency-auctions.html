How buying at a liquidation auction works

Four steps, from registering to collecting what you have won.

  1. Register to bid

    Registration is free. Manual approval and identification may be required before an account can bid, so register ahead of the sale you are watching.

  2. Read the lot and set your maximum

    Each lot carries the seller's description and photographs, its VAT treatment, and the buyer's premium on its Additional Fees tab. Add them up and decide your ceiling before the lot closes.

  3. Bid in the timed online auction

    Bidding normally remains open for several days, with the exact opening and closing times stated for each auction. In our current timed-auction format, a bid in the last ten minutes extends that lot by ten minutes, so nobody wins on the buzzer.

  4. Pay and collect

    We invoice you after the sale, payable in full by bank transfer within 24 hours. Once your payment clears we release the collection details, and you collect from the stated location by appointment.

The full buying process, costs and collection are on the buying page.

Whole-site sales

Plan the collection before you bid

Closing premises come with their own rules: fixed access windows, security requirements and a date by which the building has to be clear. Read the sale's collection information first, and it goes into your price.

On anything heavy or fixed, work out who is disconnecting it, what lifting equipment and labour the job needs, and what vehicle can get in and out. Buyers are responsible for arranging collection and transport. Any delivery option specifically stated within a lot is provided by the seller and subject to separate agreement.

Check what comes with each lot, too. Keys, manuals, service records and accessories are listed where the seller has them, and on a closure sale it pays to ask before you bid rather than after.

What is a liquidation auction?

A liquidation auction is a sale of a business's assets by open bidding, usually because the company is in liquidation, administration or receivership, or is closing down. At Universal Auctions Group these sales run as trade-only timed online auctions, so registered business buyers bid remotely and the highest bid at the close wins the lot.

Who can buy at an insolvency auction?

Any business that registers to bid. All our auctions are trade-only, so you register and bid in a business capacity. Overseas trade buyers are welcome to participate. Manual approval and identification may be required before an account can bid.

What will a lot cost me on top of the hammer price?

The buyer's premium, at the rate shown on the lot's Additional Fees tab, and VAT where it applies to that lot, which the lot description states. Collection and transport are yours to arrange, so allow for them in your maximum bid.

How quickly do I have to pay and collect?

Payment is due in full by bank transfer within 24 hours of the invoice. Collection is by appointment once your payment has cleared, within the period stated in that sale's terms. Whole-site sales can have fixed access windows and a clearance deadline, so check the sale's collection information before you bid.

Who is the seller in a liquidation sale?

Universal Auctions Group acts as auctioneer and agent for the seller. The seller, and the capacity in which they sell, such as a liquidator or administrator, varies from sale to sale and is shown in the auction information where stated.

Can I sell assets from a liquidation or closure through you?

Yes. We run sales for liquidators, administrators, receivers, lenders and business owners. The assets are sold from the premises and buyers collect after paying. Talk to us about the assets and your timetable, or read our page on asset disposal for administrators and receivers.

Are liquidation lots sold with a warranty?

Lots are sold as seen, where lying. Descriptions and photographs come from the seller, so form your own view of each lot, and ask us for anything else you need while the lot is still open.

Can I export what I buy?

Yes. Export buyers arrange their own transport and export documentation. On an export sale the VAT is charged as a deposit, and any refund is conditional on satisfactory export evidence reaching both the seller and us within 28 days of the invoice, in accordance with the auction terms. VAT on the buyer's premium is not refundable.
