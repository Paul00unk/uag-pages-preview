Who can bid?

Businesses. Our auctions are trade-only and you register and bid in a business capacity, so the sale is not a consumer contract and there is no cooling-off right.

Buyers are dealers, traders, contractors, operators, hauliers, farmers and exporters. Registering to bid may require manual approval and identification such as a driving licence or a utility bill, and requirements vary depending on which bidding platform a sale is listed on, so register in advance rather than on the day of a close.

Placing a bid is your acceptance of that sale's terms. Bids are binding and cannot be withdrawn, so do your checking before you bid and not after.

How does buying at auction with us work?

Six steps: register, check the lot, work out your maximum, bid, pay by bank transfer, then collect by appointment.

  1. Register to bid

    Create an account and register for the sale. Approval and identification may be needed. Do not leave it until the lot is closing.

  2. Read the lot, and the fees on it

    The description and photographs come from the seller. The buyer's premium and any internet surcharge that apply to that lot are shown on its Additional Fees tab, along with its VAT treatment. Read all of it before you bid.

  3. View it if you want to

    Viewing is strictly by prior appointment and we strongly recommend it. We do not inspect the lots, so your own inspection is the only inspection there is.

  4. Work out your maximum, then bid

    Add the premium, the surcharge, VAT and transport to get your real ceiling, then enter a maximum bid and let the platform work up to it in its own increments.

  5. Pay in full by bank transfer

    We raise a manual invoice. Payment is due in full within 24 hours of you receiving it. We do not accept cash, cheques or card payments.

  6. Collect by appointment

    Once your payment clears we issue the collection details. Collection is from the seller's premises, by prior appointment, within the removal window in that sale's terms.

The closing rule

How does a lot actually close?

Lots close at staggered published times, normally about ten seconds apart. If a valid bid is placed in a lot's final ten minutes, that lot's countdown resets to ten minutes from that bid, and it resets again each time another valid bid is accepted.

So a last-second bid cannot win by leaving nobody time to answer. It can still win, if no higher bid comes in during the extension. What it cannot do is buy you a time advantage, which is why setting a real maximum early beats bidding by hand at the close.

Two other things move a lot. Lots can be withdrawn or suspended under the auction terms, and we can amend published end times. And the countdown ending is not automatically a completed sale: that depends on the reserve being met or the bid otherwise being accepted. Where your bid is below the reserve it may become a provisional bid, which stays open for the seller to accept for the period stated in our terms.

What does it cost, and what will you pay?

The hammer price, a buyer's premium, any applicable internet surcharge, VAT, and your own collection or delivery costs. The premium and surcharge rates that apply to a lot are stated on that lot's Additional Fees tab, because they vary from lot to lot.

There are no standard listing fees, no card-processing charges and no buyer administration fees on a lot you pay for and collect on time. Charges do arise if you do not. Late payment can lead to interest, an administration fee and debt recovery costs, and late collection brings storage charges. The amounts are set out in the auction terms, so read them before you bid.

On VAT recovery, nothing is automatic. It depends on your VAT registration, the taxable business use of the asset, the VAT treatment of that individual lot and your holding valid VAT documentation. Some lots are sold with no VAT on the hammer price, so check the lot rather than the category, and take your own advice where the position matters.

Work your ceiling backwards, and divide the percentage charges out rather than subtracting them: they scale with whatever you bid, so a ceiling built by subtraction is always too high.

Can you view or inspect a lot before you bid?

Yes. Viewing is strictly by prior appointment and we strongly recommend it, because we do not inspect the lots and there is no condition report.

Universal Auctions Group Ltd acts solely as auctioneer and agent for the vendor. We do not see the stock. Descriptions and photographs are supplied by the seller at the time of listing, and vendor photographs may not show every angle or every defect. Mileage and hour readings are not warranted unless the listing expressly says so, and stock images are sometimes used where a sale holds several similar lots.

On vehicles we may carry out a basic vehicle data check. That is not a mechanical inspection, a title guarantee or a substitute for your own enquiries, and outstanding finance and title remain for you to investigate before you bid. Where you cannot inspect, price the uncertainty in rather than assuming the best case.

How do you collect, and what does transport cost?

Collection is from the seller's premises, by prior appointment, once your payment has cleared. We do not offer delivery, but we can obtain a no-obligation transport quotation on any lot.

Send us the lot number and your postcode while bidding is still open and we will come back with a figure, so transport is in your ceiling rather than a surprise afterwards. Collection and transport remain your responsibility unless separately agreed. The collection instructions are set by the seller, and collection may be refused if no appointment has been made.

Loading equipment and assistance at the seller's site are not guaranteed. Before you bid, establish who is responsible for loading, what equipment is available, who will operate it and whether any charge applies. On anything heavy, give your haulier accurate weights and dimensions: the operator remains responsible for choosing the right vehicle, the route and any abnormal-load notifications.

Storage charges apply if a lot is not collected in time. They run per lot per day, including weekends and bank holidays, and must be settled before a lot is released. A lot left long enough can be treated as abandoned, with ownership passing to us and no refund. The grace period, the daily rate and the abandonment period are all in the auction terms.

Can overseas buyers bid and export?

Yes. Online bidding means you do not need to be in the UK to buy, and we are EORI registered. Being EORI registered does not make us the exporter, the exporter of record, your customs agent or your transport provider.

On an export sale the VAT is charged as a refundable deposit rather than waived at the point of sale. It is refunded once you provide acceptable written evidence of export within 28 days of the invoice date. If acceptable evidence does not arrive in time the deposit is forfeited, and VAT charged on the buyer's premium cannot be refunded in any circumstances.

We do not arrange shipping or export documentation. Export remains your responsibility and is subject to the destination, the type of asset, sanctions, export controls, customs requirements and port arrangements. Tell us before the sale if you are exporting, so the paperwork and the timings are agreed while there is still time.

Questions buyers ask before they bid

Can a lot be withdrawn from a sale?

Yes. Lots can be withdrawn or suspended under the auction terms before or during a sale, and we can amend published end times. It is worth having a second lot in mind rather than pinning a week's plan on one.

Do you check for outstanding finance on vehicles?

We may carry out a basic vehicle data check. That is not a mechanical inspection, a title guarantee or a substitute for your own enquiries. Outstanding finance can affect legal title and whether the seller has authority to transfer it, so carry out your own finance and title checks on any lot you are serious about.

Is there a condition report for each lot?

No. We do not inspect the lots and we produce no condition report or mechanical appraisal. Lots are offered under the applicable auction terms using the seller's own descriptions and photographs. That is exactly why viewing is strongly recommended.

Is a deposit or a card needed to register?

No deposit is taken at registration under our own sale terms, but do not treat that as a blanket rule: registration may require approval and identification, and requirements vary by bidding platform. An export VAT deposit does apply where you are buying to export.

What happens if I win and then cannot pay?

Payment is due in full by bank transfer within 24 hours of the invoice. Failure to pay without prior agreement is a breach of contract: the lot is withdrawn from you, a buyer's premium invoice is still issued, and interest, an administration fee and debt recovery costs can follow, as set out in the auction terms. A bid is a commitment, so decide your number before the close.

Can I bid on several similar lots and keep the one I want?

No. Every winning bid is separately binding and generally cannot be withdrawn, so leaving maximum bids on three similar lots risks winning and having to complete on all three. Bid on the first, then move to the next only once you have lost it.

What if I cannot collect straight away?

Tell us before the sale ends if collection will be tight. Storage is charged per lot per day once the grace period passes and must be settled before a lot is released, and a lot left uncollected long enough may be treated as abandoned with no refund. The periods and the rate are in the auction terms.
