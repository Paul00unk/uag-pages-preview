Where the machinery comes from

Farm dispersals when a farmer retires or scales back, contractors changing machines between seasons, dealers moving on trade-ins, estates, lenders and receivers.

A dispersal can put a farm's tractors, implements and trailers into the same auction, so you can buy several machines from one farm.

Where the seller supplies them, listings include hours, transmission, linkage and PTO details, hydraulic services, tyres and cab specification, with photographs of each machine.

How buying at an agricultural equipment auction works

Four steps, from registering to collecting what you have won.

  1. Register to bid

    Registration is free. Manual approval and identification may be required before an account can bid, so register ahead of the sale you are watching.

  2. Read the lot and set your maximum

    Each lot carries the seller's description and photographs, with hours and specification where supplied, its VAT treatment, and the buyer's premium on its Additional Fees tab. Add them up and decide your ceiling before the lot closes.

  3. Bid in the timed online auction

    Bidding normally remains open for several days, with the exact opening and closing times stated for each auction. In our current timed-auction format, a bid in the last ten minutes extends that lot by ten minutes, so nobody wins on the buzzer.

  4. Pay and collect

    We invoice you after the sale, payable in full by bank transfer within 24 hours. Once your payment clears we release the collection details, and you collect from the stated location by appointment.

Before you bid

Check the machine, the documents and the haul

Farm machinery is big, wide and may be parked on soft ground. Price the haulage and the access into your bid alongside the machine.

Viewings are available by appointment and are strongly recommended. Buyers should satisfy themselves about condition, specification and suitability before bidding. Universal Auctions Group acts as auctioneer and agent for the seller.

Treat hours and working status as the seller's information unless the lot states otherwise, and compare them with service records, photographs and visible wear on tyres, linkage, PTO and hydraulics. Where brakes, lighting, road registration, weights or sprayer testing matter to you, check the documents before you bid.

Check the lot's location, access, ground conditions and loading facilities, and the machine's width, height and weight. Collection is by appointment after cleared payment. Buyers are responsible for arranging collection and transport. Any delivery option specifically stated within a lot is provided by the seller and subject to separate agreement.

Agricultural equipment auction questions

The questions buyers and sellers ask before their first farm machinery sale. For anything else, get in touch.

What types of agricultural machinery can you buy at auction?

Tractors, telehandlers and loaders, combines and forage harvesters, balers, mowers and other grassland equipment, ploughs, cultivators and drills, trailers, sprayers and specialist equipment. What comes up depends on the season and what sellers consign, so watch each sale.

Can dealers and exporters buy at your farm machinery auctions?

Yes. All our auctions are trade-only, and dealers, contractors, farmers and overseas trade buyers are welcome to participate, registering and bidding in a business capacity. Manual approval and identification may be required before an account can bid.

How does a farm dispersal sale work online?

The machinery stays on the farm and is listed as separate lots in a timed online auction. Bidding normally remains open for several days, buyers pay by bank transfer after the sale, and each buyer collects from the farm by appointment once payment has cleared.

What will a lot cost me on top of the hammer price?

A 20% buyer's premium plus VAT is payable in addition to the hammer price. VAT on the hammer price applies where stated in the lot description. Buyers must also allow for collection and transport costs.

How quickly do I have to pay and collect?

Payment is due in full by bank transfer within 24 hours of the invoice. Collection is by appointment once your payment has cleared, within the period stated in that sale's terms. Sites can have limited access, so check the collection details before you bid.

Can I export farm machinery I buy?

Yes. Export buyers arrange their own transport and export documentation. On an export sale the VAT is charged as a deposit, and any refund is conditional on satisfactory export evidence reaching both the seller and us within 28 days of the invoice, in accordance with the auction terms. VAT on the buyer's premium is not refundable.

Can I sell farm machinery through you?

Yes. We run sales for farmers, contractors, estates, dealers, lenders and receivers, from a single tractor to a full dispersal. Machinery is sold from the seller's premises or the location stated in the lot details, and buyers collect after paying. Talk to us about what you have and your timetable.

Is farm machinery sold with a warranty?

Lots are sold as seen, where lying. Descriptions and photographs come from the seller, so form your own view of each machine, and ask us for anything else you need while the lot is still open.
